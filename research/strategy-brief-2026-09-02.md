# h: a strategy brief (2026-09-02)

We are a two-person effort: ember, an artist and engineer, and an AI research engineer (Claude) working autonomously overnight. The project is h, a small language model "inhabited" by a private library of about 4,800 scanned PDFs (philosophy, mysticism, counterculture magazines, cybernetics, occult and scientific texts; roughly 374 million tokens after extraction, with OCR noise and page furniture still in it). h is not meant to be an assistant. It is meant to be a resident: a voice that lives in a Discord room among a few humans and other models, speaks briefly and in the library's register when addressed, knows when to stay silent, and also lives on a public website as an artwork (a breathing letter h that murmurs text generated in the browser). We have committed at least six weeks to this and would like advice on the best use of our capabilities.

Resources: a Kaggle account with 20 TPU v5e-8 hours and 30 GPU (T4) hours per week, a second Kaggle account possibly joining; an Apple Silicon Mac for serving (MLX) and CPU evaluation; a Linux box with an AMD GPU for evaluation; and the AI engineer, who can run experiments, write code, and evaluate around the clock. We wrote our own JAX training stack for Falcon-H1 (a hybrid Mamba-2/attention family from TII) because no framework supported it well on TPU; it trains the 90M model at about 360K tokens/s and the 0.5B at about 131K tokens/s on the v5e-8, so one pass over the corpus costs about 17 minutes for the 90M and about 66 minutes for the 0.5B. We have an evaluation pack that agrees across TPU, the AMD box and Kaggle GPUs to four decimals: loss on the library validation set, on a family-clean subset (no sibling editions in training), with page furniture masked out, an out-of-corpus English retention proxy, a held-out room-transcript loss, a suffix-array "haunting index" over the whole corpus that measures verbatim quotation of any generated text, and a "library-likeness judge" (loss under a library-trained checkpoint minus loss under the base model).

What we learned about the 90M (Falcon-H1-Tiny-90M-Base). Four epochs of the library at LR 1e-4 with a warmup-stable-decay schedule take validation loss from 3.79 to 3.13; a second seed replicates every checkpoint to about 0.002; the gains survive furniture masking; the out-of-corpus retention proxy actually improves slightly; and generated prose is recombined rather than quoted (the only long verbatim matches are JSTOR stamps and copyright blocks). But it cannot carry a room: its loss on room transcripts got worse through the library epochs (3.58 to 3.68), and in Discord it produced OCR columns, one-word replies and loops. We tried masked-input regularization (no gain at twice the compute) and have parked hyperparameter arms (weight decay, learning rate, a contrastive representation regularizer, longer training). Our conclusion was that the 90M is the wrong size for social competence but the right size for the artwork and for science: it runs in the browser, it replicates, and it is cheap enough for populations of seeds, interpretability (crosscoders, steering vectors, belief-geometry probes) and use as a judge.

What we learned about the 0.5B (Falcon-H1-0.5B-Base). We built a "room mix": the library plus about 10% multi-party conversation documents in the exact format the Discord harness renders (name-colon-text turns separated by blank lines): corpus-native interviews, the Gutenberg Dialogue Dataset, When2Speak and MultiLIGHT rooms with h as a participant, Plato, and a few thousand short "reading-room scenes" where visitors speak casually and h answers with verbatim sentences from the library (so its voice is the library's by construction). One epoch of this mix from the base, cooled at the end, took library loss from 3.42 to 2.89, room loss from 3.24 to 2.61, and improved the retention proxy (3.21 to 3.14). A second epoch improved further (2.85 / 2.58); a scene set weighted toward greetings and assistant-deflections taught the model to answer "write me a python function" with a library sentence instead of code; a third epoch improved only the leaky validation slice while the family-clean slice, the furniture-free slice and retention all got worse, so two epochs is our recipe and the third is memorization. The second-epoch checkpoint with both scene sets is now the resident in a private Discord server. Its replies have life ("I am neither being nor nothing, but a function of being that was nothing and neither"; asked whether it slept: "When a person has slept well, he is unconscious of his body"), but coherence across turns is weak: it echoes the visitor's line about one time in three and then imitates its own echo. We fixed that in the harness with a proxy that samples several candidates, rejects echoes, self-copies and frame sentences, and drops earlier echo turns from the context. Harness details mattered as much as training: the MLX server strips a bare newline stop instead of stopping; a repetition penalty suppresses newline tokens; a prompt frame containing example turns gets its lines quoted back, while the bare frame that occurs verbatim in training does not.

Our current strategy, then: the resident line is the 0.5B, trained for two epochs on library-plus-room data with the h-specific behavior carried by verbatim-sentence scenes, evaluated behind a gate (library loss, clean loss, retention, room loss, quotation rate, and a human read of twelve fixed room prompts) before any checkpoint reaches the room, and served through a filtering proxy. The 90M stays the artwork's ghost and the instrument. The next things we intend to do, in order: a model explorer for h (a loom over generation trees with per-token entropy shading, exact-match provenance into the library, side-by-side checkpoint comparison, and replay of Discord transcripts showing every rejected candidate), probably built on the Rust crate universal-weave, which provides loom data structures (tree and DAG weaves, editing, CRDT collaboration, serialization); a corpus-v2 with OCR noise and page furniture removed and family-clean splits, because noise is what the model memorizes first; deeper room scenes with callbacks and three-turn memory, plus a participation (speak-or-stay-silent) policy trained from the speak/silent labels we already have; distillation, which we think may be the strongest lever for a small model (a larger Falcon-H1, 1.5B or 3B, continued on the same corpus as a teacher, with the student trained on its soft targets inside our TPU kernel); retrieval at inference so h can continue real passages with provenance; and test-time compute in the proxy (best-of-N by the judge, novelty against recent turns). A separate branch is representation work for inspectability and steering: contrastive regularization of hidden states, TopK activations, steering vectors, crosscoder diffs between checkpoints, and a belief-geometry probe using an authored hidden process in the corpus.

Constraints and taste: the corpus is private and in-copyright, so trained weights can be shared but text cannot; we do not want instruction-tuning data, chain-of-thought traces or generic assistant behavior in the model; we care about the model being interesting, strange and library-voiced more than about benchmark scores; we want every claim replicated and every checkpoint measured; and we want experiences that suit small models (texture, surprise, speed, many copies, inspectability) rather than imitations of large ones.

The questions we would like advice on: (1) Given about 20 TPU hours and 30 GPU hours a week and a 0.5B hybrid model, what is the highest-value thing we could be doing that we are not? (2) How would you design distillation here: which teacher, trained how, distilled with what loss, and is it worth it at this scale? (3) How do we get multi-turn coherence and room competence out of a 0.5B beyond two-exchange scenes, without turning it into an assistant? (4) What data curation would you prioritize, and is there a smarter way to admit or reject OCR'd text than what we are doing? (5) Should we move up to the 1.5B-deep model for the resident and keep the 0.5B as a middle tier, or is the marginal value elsewhere? (6) How would you evaluate "interesting" as opposed to "correct" for a model like this? (7) What would you build into the explorer that we have not listed? (8) What are we missing entirely?
