### Response-span weighting: the answer

Three arms at matched compute to e2-v3 (second epoch on v1.3 from the e1 pre-cooldown), with per-token loss
weights over library / room-other / h-utterance / h-label classes. All held-out numbers below are on the same
512 room sequences and the same sidecar (hbox), so they are comparable:

| arm | weights (lib, room, h, label) | library (TPU) | room slice | h-span | other room | echo rate (bank) |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| e2-v3 | 1, 1, 1, 1 | 2.852 | 2.581 | 2.762 | 2.578 | 0.34 |
| h ×8 | 1, 1, 8, 8 | 2.864 | 2.599 | 2.763 | 2.597 | 0.32 |
| visitors ×0.25 | 1, 0.25, 4, 4 | 2.854 | 2.635 | 2.747 | 2.634 | 0.37 |
| visitors ×0 | 1, 0, 4, 4 | 2.855 | 2.882 | 2.748 | 2.888 | 0.35 |

Weighting h's lines does not lower their held-out loss (2.762 → 2.747 at best, within noise) while raising the loss
on everything it down-weights; it only fits the training h-lines better (train h-loss 1.97). Removing the visitors'
lines from the loss is plainly harmful. None of the arms lowers the echo rate on the bank (0.32–0.37 against 0.34).
Context lift is evaluator-dependent and inconclusive. Decision: keep the plain objective; attack echo through data
(set C repairs and counterfactual pairs, stitched rooms) and the harness (the proxy). An earlier note in the log
claimed a 0.18-nat gain on h lines; it compared the kernel's permuted subset with hbox's and was wrong.
